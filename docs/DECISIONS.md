A 128-word latch array stays in the instruction set as the fallback | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md), `tools/check_macro_power.py` |

| 2026-11-05 | Four host link rules added to section 9.1: SCK high and low at least 2 cycles and CS high at least 3; bytes after a command's last argument are ignored; CS high or SCK low as reset ends; the host drives CS from a GPIO | Stress tests of the RTL showed a 1-cycle SCK phase or CS pulse fails once a pad edge lands a cycle late, which a 2-flop synchroniser can't prevent, and that the other three cases were unspecified | Section 9.1 and item 4 of the undefined list in section 10 |

| 2026-11-06 | Move to 8x4 tiles: 1,208,173 µm² of core area, from 902,417 on 6x4 | Jane Street confirmed 8x4 for the March shuttle, with the tile sizes final, and the planned shift units roughly double the logic. The block's pins stay in the top left corner, so the SRAM stays at the bottom left; it and the power stripes move 0.96 µm left so that the last of the 26 stripe pairs ends inside the core | `info.yaml`, `src/config.json`, `src/pdn_cfg.tcl` |
| 2026-11-06 | Move to the March shuttle's flow: tt-gds-action `ttihp27a`, tt-support-tools `main` pinned at `9d13ff3e` (2026-11-05), LibreLane 3.1.0.dev4, and IHP-Open-PDK `89d42ee6` installed with ciel 3.2.0. The template changes come by hand from the template's `main` at `4b1204ab`: the workflow tags, the tile comment in `info.yaml` and the cell model paths in `test/Makefile` | The template's `main` now targets this flow, the one the March shuttle's designs are built with. Pinning the tools commit in the workflows keeps the online checks and `scripts/setup.sh` on the same versions | `.github/workflows/`, `scripts/setup.sh` |
| 2026-11-07 | Stop the build on a max-slew or max-capacitance violation in any corner (`MAX_SLEW_VIOLATION_CORNERS`, `MAX_CAP_VIOLATION_CORNERS`), and put a buffer at each of the SRAM's 44 signal pins, placed next to the pin | These were warnings until now. The SRAM's pins have the tightest limits on the chip: 0.064 pF on each output and 0.5952 ns of slew on each input at the slow corner. In three local builds without the buffers, the slew on `A_MEN` came to 74%, 108% and 96% of its limit and the worst output load to 93%, 123% and 87%, so an enforced check would fail by chance. The flow's repair can't fix these pins: it estimates slew from drive resistance and load alone, which misses a weak gate's slow edge, and the output wires came out about 1.8 times its estimate. With the buffers, the worst SRAM input is at 27% of its limit and the worst output at 10%. The 16 output buffers are protected from repair; the 28 input buffers are not, because hold repair has to add delay in front of the SRAM's inputs | `src/pe_mem.sv`, `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-07 | Synthesize to the cell library's fanout limit of 8 (`MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT`), not the flow's default of 10 | With 10, synthesis left about 600 nets with 9 or 10 loads, and repair then brought them down to the library's 8 with 833 of the weakest buffer, 7 of them on the slowest path. With 8, the slowest path is 2.3 ns faster, the standard cells are 3,869 µm² smaller and the wire is 12% shorter | `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-07 | Leave the clock tree's fanout as the flow builds it | 97 of the 100 max-fanout warnings are on clock buffers. Every leaf is a `buf_8` driving 10 to 17 flops, loaded to under 4% of its capacitance limit, and the root buffer drives 16 buffers. The other 3 are nets with 8 cell loads plus antenna diodes added after routing. Smaller leaf clusters would add clock buffers and power for no electrical gain | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) |
| 2026-11-06 | Give each thread a shift unit (section 5.9), a general shifter for both directions, and so drop 2026-10-31's "no protocol-specific hardware" | 10 Mbit Ethernet needs a pin change every 2.5 cycles at 50 MHz, and a thread runs one instruction every 4 cycles, so firmware alone can't send or receive it. A general unit, not an Ethernet one, also serves USB, HDLC and plain serial. One per thread keeps the threads independent | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9 |
| 2026-11-07 | Run every demonstration at 50 MHz, and put CRC (any polynomial up to 32 bits) and a bit stuffer and destuffer in all four units | Low-speed USB in firmware needs exactly 50 MHz, so one clock serves every protocol. The more of the line coding, CRC and clock recovery the chip does itself, the less the firmware has to keep up with | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.5 to 5.9.7 |
| 2026-11-07 | Receive Manchester with a decoder that follows the line's phase, not one that times each edge | 802.3 allows 13.5 ns of jitter. An edge-timing decoder tolerated none of it at 40 MHz and 8.3 ns at 60 MHz. In a model of the receive path, the phase decoder tolerates far more, and loses no frame on a short cable at 40, 50 or 60 MHz | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9.6 |
| 2026-11-10 | The units time their own bits, and nothing divides instruction issue: every thread still runs one instruction every 4 cycles | Bit periods need not be a multiple of 4 cycles, while every thread's instruction timing stays exact and independent of what its unit does | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 2 and 5.9 |
| 2026-11-10 | Publish the shift units: section 5.9, `src/pe_shift.sv`, its integration into the core, its tests and the simulator's model of it | Every program and test passes on the RTL, on Icarus 12 and 13, and the simulator in `sim/` runs all 89 rows | `src/`, `test/`, `sim/` |
| 2026-11-11 | Build with `SYNTH_STRATEGY` "AREA 0"; repair slew and capacitance to 60% of each limit after placement, with a 300 µm wire limit; derate the global router's layer capacity by 25%, not 30%; and repair again after global routing, with buffering and a 300 µm limit (`RUN_POST_GRT_DESIGN_REPAIR`) | With the four shift units, the flow's default synthesis (`DELAY 4`) needs about 565,000 µm² of cells, over the area budget, and `AREA 0` about 430,000. `AREA 0` maps every driver at the smallest size, and wires between the core and the units detour on the one horizontal signal layer, so their loads came out 2 to 3.5 times the placement estimate and over the slew limit. Eight local builds narrowed it down. Two passed every enforced check; this one has no pin within 30% of its slew limit, where the other had 8 within 10% | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md), `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-11 | The CEC initiator waits for 7 bit periods of free line before every frame, ends a frame at the first block that fails, without retransmitting it, and judges a broadcast's ACK inverted. The I2C controller takes a write's data word even when the write ends early | HDMI CEC 1.4 asks 3, 5 or 7 bit periods of free line, and 7 meets all three. The host can post a failed frame again. Taking the data word keeps the mailbox in step after a NACK or a bus fault. sigrok's decode of the bus showed both programs getting this wrong | [programs/sigrok/README.md](../programs/sigrok/README.md), `programs/standard/cec.s`, `programs/standard/i2c.s` |
