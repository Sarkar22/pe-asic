| 2026-11-06 | Move to the March shuttle's flow: tt-gds-action `ttihp27a`, tt-support-tools `main` pinned at `9d13ff3e` (2026-11-05), LibreLane 3.1.0.dev4, and IHP-Open-PDK `89d42ee6` installed with ciel 3.2.0. | 2026-11-06 | Give each thread a shift unit (section 5.9), a general shifter for both directions, and so drop 2026-10-31's "no protocol-specific hardware" | 10 Mbit Ethernet needs a pin change every 2.5 cycles at 50 MHz, and a thread runs one instruction every 4 cycles, so firmware alone can't send or receive it. A general unit, not an Ethernet one, also serves USB, HDLC and plain serial. One per thread keeps the threads independent | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9 |
| 2026-11-07 | Stop the build on a max-slew or max-capacitance violation in any corner (`MAX_SLEW_VIOLATION_CORNERS`, `MAX_CAP_VIOLATION_CORNERS`), and put a buffer at each of the SRAM's 44 signal pins, placed next to the pin | These were warnings until now. The SRAM's pins have the tightest limits on the chip: 0.064 pF on each output and 0.5952 ns of slew on each input at the slow corner. In three local builds without the buffers, the slew on `A_MEN` came to 74%, 108% and 96% of its limit and the worst output load to 93%, 123% and 87%, so an enforced check would fail by chance. The flow's repair can't fix these pins: it estimates slew from drive resistance and load alone, which misses a weak gate's slow edge, and the output wires came out about 1.8 times its estimate. With the buffers, the worst SRAM input is at 27% of its limit and the worst output at 10%. The 16 output buffers are protected from repair; the 28 input buffers are not, because hold repair has to add delay in front of the SRAM's inputs | `src/pe_mem.sv`, `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-07 | Synthesize to the cell library's fanout limit of 8 (`MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT`), not the flow's default of 10 | With 10, synthesis left about 600 nets with 9 or 10 loads, and repair then brought them down to the library's 8 with 833 of the weakest buffer, 7 of them on the slowest path. With 8, the slowest path is 2.3 ns faster, the standard cells are 3,869 µm² smaller and the wire is 12% shorter | `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-07 | Leave the clock tree's fanout as the flow builds it | 97 of the 100 max-fanout warnings are on clock buffers. Every leaf is a `buf_8` driving 10 to 17 flops, loaded to under 4% of its capacitance limit, and the root buffer drives 16 buffers. The other 3 are nets with 8 cell loads plus antenna diodes added after routing. Smaller leaf clusters would add clock buffers and power for no electrical gain | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) |
| 2026-11-07 | Run every demonstration at 50 MHz, and put CRC (any polynomial up to 32 bits) and a bit stuffer and destuffer in all four units | Low-speed USB in firmware needs exactly 50 MHz, so one clock serves every protocol. The more of the line coding, CRC and clock recovery the chip does itself, the less the firmware has to keep up with | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.5 to 5.9.7 |
| 2026-11-07 | Receive Manchester with a decoder that follows the line's phase, not one that times each edge | 802.3 allows 13.5 ns of jitter. An edge-timing decoder tolerated none of it at 40 MHz and 8.3 ns at 60 MHz. In a model of the receive path, the phase decoder tolerates far more, and loses no frame on a short cable at 40, 50 or 60 MHz | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9.6 |
| 2026-11-10 | The units time their own bits, and nothing divides instruction issue: every thread still runs one instruction every 4 cycles | Bit periods need not be a multiple of 4 cycles, while every thread's instruction timing stays exact and independent of what its unit does | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 2 and 5.9 |
| 2026-11-10 | Publish the shift units: section 5.9, `src/pe_shift.sv`, its integration into the core, its tests and the simulator's model of it | Every program and test passes on the RTL, on Icarus 12 and 13, and the simulator in `sim/` runs all 89 rows | `src/`, `test/`, `sim/` |
| 2026-11-11 | Build with `SYNTH_STRATEGY` "AREA 0"; repair slew and capacitance to 60% of each limit after placement, with a 300 µm wire limit; derate the global router's layer capacity by 25%, not 30%; and repair again after global routing, with buffering and a 300 µm limit (`RUN_POST_GRT_DESIGN_REPAIR`) | With the four shift units, the flow's default synthesis (`DELAY 4`) needs about 565,000 µm² of cells, over the area budget, and `AREA 0` about 430,000. `AREA 0` maps every driver at the smallest size, and wires between the core and the units detour on the one horizontal signal layer, so their loads came out 2 to 3.5 times the placement estimate and over the slew limit. Eight local builds narrowed it down. Two passed every enforced check; this one has no pin within 30% of its slew limit, where the other had 8 within 10% | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md), `src/config.json` |
| 2026-11-11 | The CEC initiator waits for 7 bit periods of free line before every frame, ends a frame at the first block that fails, without retransmitting it, and judges a broadcast's ACK inverted. The I2C controller takes a write's data word even when the write ends early | HDMI CEC 1.4 asks 3, 5 or 7 bit periods of free line, and 7 meets all three. The host can post a failed frame again. Taking the data word keeps the mailbox in step after a NACK or a bus fault. sigrok's decode of the bus showed both programs getting this wrong | [programs/sigrok/README.md](../programs/sigrok/README.md), `programs/standard/cec.s`, `programs/standard/i2c.s` |
| 2026-11-11 | Publish the simulator compiled from the specification (`sim/docsim.py`), the second simulator it builds on, the check of the document's claims about itself, and a comparison of all three simulators on every cycle | So anyone can rerun the cross-check between the document and the simulators, not only read about it | [sim/README.md](../sim/README.md) |
| 2026-11-16 | Run the 10BASE-T and USB demonstrations end to end now, on the RTL and the gate-level netlist, not after the hardware freeze; and let the RTL change before the freeze wherever a test or a build needs it | A shortfall in the hardware can be fixed only before the freeze. The firmware for both had been planned for after it | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md), `programs/advanced/eth_arp.s`, `programs/advanced/usb_enum.s`, `test_eth_arp` and `test_usb_enumerate` in `test/test.py` |
| 2026-11-20 | Fix the hardware where a test shows it short of a standard, rather than document the limit: half-cycle timing for 10BASE-T's receive jitter and transmit edges, and USB's dribble bits taken by the units | The end-to-end tests found the 10BASE-T receiver losing frames inside 802.3's ±13.5 ns of jitter, its transmit edges up to 5 ns off the grid, and a USB packet with a dribble bit failing its CRC check. A firmware way round took one dribble bit, where USB 2.0 7.1.9.1 allows up to six bit times | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.5 to 5.9.7 and 7.4 |
| 2026-11-20 | Time the 10BASE-T pins to the half cycle with the cell library's latches, and no flop on the falling edge: each input pad is also sampled at the falling edge through a pair of latches, and each shift output comes from a flop P XOR a flop N seen through a latch open while the clock is low. THALF sends on that grid and RKIND 3 decodes from both samples | A half bit of 10BASE-T is 2.5 cycles at 50 MHz, and a pulse can fall between two rising-edge samples. Latches give the second edge without a second clock domain, and the output can't glitch, because the two inputs of the XOR never change in the same half cycle | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 3.1, 5.2 (implementation note), 5.9.5 and 5.9.6; [formal/README.md](../formal/README.md), properties 1b, 4 and 5 |
| 2026-11-20 | I2S and TDM on the units as they are, with no frame pin | One unit makes SCK and WS (FS) from a pattern table, and units started on one cycle by `wrd` and `tick 0` share one grid, so the frame needs no new hardware | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 11.2 (`i2s_ctl`, `i2s_tgt`) |
| 2026-11-21 | USB's byte end keeps every bit when a stuff violation came: REOP drops 1 to 6 ones after the last whole byte only if no seven 1s in a row result | USB 2.0 7.1.9.1 counts seven 1s in a row as a bit stuffing error, not a dribble | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.7 (the byte end) and 11.5 |
| 2026-11-21 | A byte end for 802.3: with RKIND 3 and REOP, a frame that ends 1 to 7 bits after its last whole octet ends at that octet, and its FCS is checked there | IEEE 802.3 4.2.9 truncates a received frame to whole octets before its FCS check, so the receiver dropped frames a standard MAC keeps | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9.7 (the byte end of a Manchester frame); `test_eth_dribble` in `test/test.py` |
| 2026-11-22 | A reception of RKIND 3 keeps the *p*, RGAIN and RACQ it started with (RXP3, RXRG, RXRA); a new RXPER or RXOPT acts on them from the next reception | A write of either in mid-reception could make the decoder's code open two windows in one cycle. Held values also keep the decoder inside the ranges its proofs rest on | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 5.9.6 (Start) and section 10, defined 8; [formal/README.md](../formal/README.md), property 7c |
| 2026-11-22 | Two CAN rules in the units: with RESYNC and RACQ, timed samples resynchronize only on edges to HLVL, recessive to dominant; with SMODE 2, a stuff violation ends the reception at the violating bit | Bosch CAN 2.0 part A resynchronizes on recessive-to-dominant edges only (its synchronization rules) and starts the error flag at the bit after a stuff error (error signalling). Without the first, a third node lost the winner's frame when two others arbitrated out of step | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.6, 5.9.7, 7.4 and 11.5; `test_can_third_node` and `test_can_stuff_error_flag` in `test/test.py` |
| 2026-11-23 | The 10BASE-T decoder (RKIND 3, section 5.9.6): a candidate keeps its phase and gives the bit only *p*/8 after the predicted clock edge unless an edge comes first; the edge after a bit-boundary edge is the clock edge; an edge in the half step that decides overrides only a candidate more than *p*/8 early; no pull in a cycle with an edge in each half. A decoder that waits about a bit before it decides was studied and not taken | It loses no frame at 802.3's ±13.5 ns with random jitter, the fixed patterns and a cable's jitter, nor, with a 50% clock, at B.4.1's jitter budget for a short link (sections 7.3 and 7.4); it fits the area and 50 MHz timing, and it has proofs. The waiting decoder needed several times the area allowed for it and missed 50 MHz | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 5.9.6, 7.3 and 7.4; [formal/README.md](../formal/README.md), properties 7 to 7c |
| 2026-11-23 | Equalize the 10BASE-T transmitter on the board, not in the chip: two equal legs from shift outputs A and B, a transformer and pad, a bridged-T shelf and a 5-pole low-pass (section 7.3) | IEEE 802.3 14.3.1.2.1 asks the transmitter to equalize. A passive network after the units' three-level drive meets the template, the harmonic limit and the return loss, so no chip feature is needed | [ISA.md](ISA.md), section 7.3, "10BASE-T transmit network" |
| 2026-11-23 | Route the clock tree on Metal3 and Metal4 only (`RT_CLOCK_MIN_LAYER`), and derate the global router's layers by 20%, not 25% (`GRT_ADJUSTMENT`) | With the final decoder the layout is denser: detailed routing stalled on shorts against the clock trunks on Metal2, and at 25% the last global route sent a few nets round past the slew limit after the last repair. | 2026-11-26 | Accept 59.2% utilization, over the 58% placement target | Every enforced check passes, and a 58.07% build had passed Tiny Tapeout's precheck online | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md); gds runs 44964490885 (`5a01610`) and 45941778685 (`04f143e`) |
| 2026-11-26 | Accept the 10BASE-T receiver's limits as sections 7.3 and 7.4 state them: it loses frames to data-dependent jitter of the opposite sign past the bounds section 7.3 gives, to steps of 21 ns or more, to spikes and to some frames with 7 excess bits; and three cases hold only with a clock close to 50% duty: the opposite sign at 10 ns, 20 ns steps and IEEE 802.3 B.4.1's split for a short link | Inside 802.3's own budget split, with a 50% clock, the receiver loses no frame, and a decoder that does better did not fit (2026-11-23) | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 7.3 and 7.4 |
| 2026-11-26 | State the remaining small limits instead of changing the hardware: the LFSR starts only from all ones; SPI target words framed by CS run only to 3.57 MHz, and MISO, a `uo` pin, isn't released while CS is high; the CAN node has no error counters or error-passive state and takes extended frames for errors. A card whose output delay put its start bit in the cycle a hunt starts would have lost it | [STATUS.md](STATUS.md), open items; [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 7.4, 11.2 and 11.5 |
| 2026-11-27 | Fix SD's start bit and CAN's error flag in firmware, with no RTL change: an SD reception starts at a commit before its start bit, with the start bit as a 1-bit sync pattern; the CAN node writes RXCNT and waits for the next word sooner, so every stuff error's flag starts in the next bit, at 500 kbit/s and 1 Mbit/s | A hunt takes no sample in the cycle it starts, so a card whose output delay, with pads and board, was H − 1 to H cycles at half clock H lost the start bit. At 1 Mbit/s some flags started a bit late, and a stuff error in SOF to DLC bit 3 got none | [ISA.md](ISA.md), sections 7.4, 11.2 (`sd_unit`, `can_unit`) and 11.5; `programs/bench/sd_unit.py`, `programs/bench/can_unit.py` |
